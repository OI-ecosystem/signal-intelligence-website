30th Celebration’s Chase Mew Is Down 87% Since Launch. Two Earlier Hyped Sets Took Very Different Paths.

26 Nov 2026 5 min read Signal Intelligence Research

The 30th Celebration set has fallen hard on the secondary market since its 16 September release: by 19 November its Futuristic Rare Mew ex was tracked at $28.76, 87% below its release-day price, and the standard Elite Trainer Box was 30% lower. Pokémon has seen hyped launches cool before, and the two clearest precedents, Celebrations in 2021 and Prismatic Evolutions in 2025, show that what followed a fall varied a great deal and played out over months or years.

What is happening now

The Pokémon TCG: 30th Celebration set went on sale worldwide on 16 September 2026. The run-up was intense. Kotaku reported that the set’s Future Rare Mew had been listed for over $3,000 before launch and had routinely sold for between $850 and $1,400 on eBay in late August and early September. Five days after release, the same card was routinely listed for around $120.

Two things explain most of the fall, according to the coverage at the time. The first is supply: Kotaku described 30th Celebration as having “the largest print of any Pokémon set to date”, with printing set to continue for many months. The second is pull rates. Special Illustration Rares, which had appeared in around 1 in 80 packs in recent sets, were turning up in better than 1 in 20, according to Kotaku. More hits per pack means more copies of each chase card reach the market.

Sealed product followed a similar but gentler path. The Elite Trainer Box carries an MSRP of $49.99, and ICv2 reported that some sold for just over $300 on eBay in the first days after release. By 19 November, TCGinvest’s price series put the standard Elite Trainer Box at $110.58, 30% below its release-day level, the Booster Bundle 48% lower and the Futuristic Rare Mew ex at $28.76, down 87%. Delta Reign, the next main expansion, followed on 6 November.

So the question many collectors are asking is a fair one: has a heavily hyped Pokémon set fallen like this before, and what came next?

Precedent one: Celebrations, 2021

The closest comparison is the last anniversary set. Celebrations, for the 25th anniversary, was released on 8 October 2021 in the middle of a collecting boom, with an Elite Trainer Box at $49.99. Supply caught up quickly: Walmart was still restocking the box at about $50 in November and December 2021, according to TCGinvest.

The chase singles then fell for a long time. TCGinvest’s figures show the Classic Collection Charizard launching near $157 and falling to $55 by early 2024, a 65% decline, while the Umbreon Gold Star went from $56 to $13, down 77%. Both traded below their launch prices for nearly three years. By June 2026 they were at $208 and $102.

The sealed boxes took a different route. In TCGinvest’s monthly series, built from PriceCharting’s completed sales, the Celebrations Elite Trainer Box sat between $63 and $74 for three years, from 2022 to late 2024. It then rose to $122 in April 2025, $219 in October 2025 and $330 in April 2026. Most of that rise came three or more years after release.

Precedent two: Prismatic Evolutions, 2025

The second precedent is a shortage rather than a flood. Prismatic Evolutions launched in January 2025 and sold out almost everywhere. Kotaku reported on 14 January 2025 that a $60 Elite Trainer Box had a resale market price of $400, and The Pokémon Company International said it was working to print more of the affected products “as quickly as possible and at maximum capacity”.

The reprints arrived over the following months, and prices cooled as they did. In TCGinvest’s daily TCGplayer series, the Elite Trainer Box was $135.79 on 1 April 2025, $118.06 on 1 May and $95.90 on 9 June, a 29% fall across the restock window; GameStop restocked the box on 10 May. The box later climbed to $212.83 on 23 March 2026 before easing to $140.40 by late September 2026, as restocks continued into 2026.

What was similar, and what was different

All three sets arrived with high expectations, and in each case prices came down once buyers could see how much product, and how many chase cards, would actually reach the market.

The differences matter at least as much:

  • Why prices fell. Celebrations and 30th Celebration cooled as plentiful supply met demand. Prismatic Evolutions cooled because a shortage was eased by reprints.
  • Pull rates. 30th Celebration’s unusually generous hit rates put more chase cards into circulation than a typical set, which weighs on singles in a way the earlier sets did not face to the same degree.
  • Singles versus sealed. In 2021, the singles and the sealed boxes behaved very differently over the next five years. A fall in one did not tell you what the other would do.
  • Time. The Celebrations box was broadly flat for three years before it rose. Where prices later rose, as with the Celebrations box and the Prismatic Evolutions box in early 2026, the rise came many months or years after the fall.
  • Ongoing printing. 30th Celebration is still being printed, so its supply is not yet fixed. Celebrations and Prismatic Evolutions also saw restocks after launch, but the total size of each print is not public.

What to watch

For anyone following the set, the useful signals are factual rather than predictive. How long does printing continue, and do retail restocks keep pace through the holiday season? Do the chase singles keep falling as more packs are opened, or does trading settle into a range? Does sealed product keep trading below its release-day price, as the Celebrations box traded near its shelf price for years?

The precedents show that a sharp fall after a hyped launch has happened before, and that what followed ranged from years of flat prices to a later rise driven by the wider market. They do not show what this set will do. Different supply, pull rates and market conditions mean 2026 may not follow 2021 or 2025 at all.
