Gold Has Fallen Far From Its January Record. Here Is What Followed Its Last Two Peaks.

9 Nov 2026 4 min read Signal Intelligence Research

Gold has been here before: after its peaks in January 1980 and September 2011, it did not set a new record until January 2008 and July 2020. Spot gold is now near $4,070, well below January's record of about $5,595, and the speed and causes of this year's swing differ from both precedents in ways worth understanding.

Gold set a record on 29 January 2026, when spot prices touched $5,594.82 an ounce, according to Reuters. The next day it fell hard. Spot gold closed 8.9% lower at $4,894.23, after an intraday drop of more than 12%, which Bloomberg described as the biggest intraday decline since the early 1980s. The trigger was a rebound in the dollar on reports, later confirmed, that Kevin Warsh would be nominated to chair the Federal Reserve, a choice markets read as hawkish.

The slide did not stop there. Bloomberg reported on 5 November that gold was still down by more than a fifth since the US-Iran war erupted in late February, as the jump in energy prices pushed central banks towards tighter policy. Reuters recorded a one-year low of $3,869.85 on 30 October.

The first week of November brought a rebound. On 4 November gold rose about 3%, to around $4,046.74, as the dollar slid after Democratic gains in the US midterm elections. On 5 November it reached $4,146.63, its highest since 20 October, on the same day the Bank of England voted 8–1 to raise Bank Rate by 0.25 percentage points to 4%. By 6 November spot gold was at $4,070.59, up about 5% for the week so far, according to Reuters, with markets pricing a 70% chance of a Federal Reserve rate rise in December.

So the question many readers are asking is a simple one: has gold fallen this far from a record before, and what came next? It has, twice since 1980, and both times the wait for a new record was long.

1980: a record that stood for 28 years

Gold reached $850 an ounce at the London afternoon fix on 21 January 1980. It did not hold. Gold made its low on 21 June 1982, before a rally of 65% took it to $488.50 later that year, according to the Northern Miner, still far below the 1980 peak.

The longer arc was harder still. In July 1999 gold touched what the London Bullion Market Association called a historic low of $252.80. The 1980 record was not broken in nominal terms until 2 January 2008, when gold reached $859.20, a high that AFP reported as a 28-year record.

2011: a crash in 2013, and a wait until 2020

The second precedent is more recent. Gold climbed to an LBMA afternoon price of $1,895.0 on 5 September 2011, and to an intraday high of $1,921.2 the following day, according to the World Gold Council.

The decline that followed included one of the sharpest single days in the metal's history. On 15 April 2013 US gold futures settled down $140.30 at $1,361.10, a fall of more than 9%, which CNBC reported as the biggest percentage decrease since 17 March 1980. Reuters put the fall in spot gold at 8.9%. Prices kept falling, reaching a near-six-year low of $1,045.85 in December 2015.

Gold did not set a new record until 28 July 2020, when the World Gold Council recorded an LBMA afternoon price of $1,940.9. On 7 August 2020 spot gold reached $2,072.50, according to Reuters. The 2011 record had stood from September 2011 to July 2020.

What is similar, and what is different

  • Interest rates. This year's reports tie gold's moves closely to interest-rate expectations: the January fall followed the Warsh news, the later decline has been linked to tighter policy after the energy shock, and markets are now pricing a possible Fed rise in December. The precedents show what a long period below a record looks like; they do not show that today's causes are the same.
  • Speed. The 1980 and 2011 declines unfolded over years. This year, a fall of 8.9% came the day after the record, and a one-year low followed by late October.
  • Distance. At its 1999 low gold was at $252.80 against a 1980 peak of $850, and in December 2015 at $1,045.85 against a 2011 peak of $1,921.2. At $4,070.59 on 6 November, against January's $5,594.82, the gap so far is narrower than at either of those lows.
  • Context. The 2026 record came during what Reuters called a safe-haven rush and was followed by a war and an energy price spike. Each earlier peak had its own setting, so the comparison is about the shape of what followed, not a claim that history will repeat.

What to watch

The useful questions are factual ones. Does the Federal Reserve raise rates in December, as markets were pricing on 6 November? Does the dollar keep weakening after the midterms? And do other central banks follow the Bank of England's move this month?

The precedents do not tell us how this cycle will end. They show only that, twice before, a record was followed by a long period below it, and that the length of that period depended on rates, inflation and events that no one could see at the peak.
