1st Edition Charizard Has Reportedly Passed $1 Million. Its Last Record Stood for Almost Four Years.

22 Nov 2026 4 min read Signal Intelligence Research

A PSA 10 1st Edition Base Set Charizard was reported in October 2026 to have sold for $1,385,274, two and a half times the $550,000 record set only ten months earlier. The card has been through a run like this once before: it climbed from under $20,000 in 2017 to $420,000 in March 2022, and that record then stood for almost four years.

On 9 October 2026, Vice reported that a 1999 Pokémon Base Set 1st Edition holographic Charizard graded PSA 10 had sold for $1,385,274, a record for the card. The figure was made public on X by Jeremy Padawer, president of the toy maker Jazwares; Vice did not name the platform or the date of the sale.

A second result followed two weeks later. Fanatics Collect's October Premier Auction closed a PSA 10 1st Edition Charizard at $1,380,000 on 23 October 2026, a $1,150,000 winning bid plus a 20 percent buyer's premium. The same graded copy had sold on Fanatics Collect for $480,000 in March 2026. When checked shortly after the close, Fanatics' sold records listed the result as unpaid, so it should be read as a hammer price rather than a completed sale.

The scale of the move is the point. Through June 2026, sales of this card ran between $456,000 and $528,000, according to sales data compiled by UltraBall. In December 2025, Heritage Auctions sold a copy for $550,000, which was then the record. The reported October price is about two and a half times that.

A market running hot at the top

The Charizard result sits inside a wider surge in high-end Pokémon sales. UltraBall counted 852 Pokémon card sales of $100,000 or more between 1 January and 16 November 2026, worth a combined $248 million, and 25 sales of $1 million or more. On Fanatics Collect alone, sales of $100,000 or more rose from 28 in August to 90 in September and 198 in October. UltraBall notes that part of the jump reflects when individual marketplaces entered its data, so the counts overstate the change somewhat.

Supply at the top grade is small. The collectibles news site cllct put the number of PSA-graded 1st Edition Base Set Charizards at 5,325 in December 2025, of which 124 were PSA 10. Population counts quoted between 2022 and 2026 range from 121 to 125, which suggests very few new top-grade copies have appeared in four years.

The precedent: the 2020 to 2022 run

This is the second time in six years that this card has re-priced sharply. According to Card Ladder data cited by cllct, a PSA 10 1st Edition Charizard sold for under $20,000 in 2017 and for more than $310,000 in 2021, with no publicly recorded sales in between.

The climb was fast and very public. In October 2020, the rapper Logic paid about $220,000 for a PSA 10 copy. A copy sold on eBay for $311,800 in March 2021. On 19 March 2022, PWCC Auctions sold one for $420,000, which Hypebeast described at the time as the highest price ever paid for a Base Set Charizard. PWCC said 122 copies had received a PSA 10.

What came next is the useful part. The $420,000 record was not beaten until Heritage's $550,000 sale in December 2025, a gap of almost four years. In between, the mood around the card turned. In September 2023, Wargamer reported a CGC Gem Mint 10 copy selling for $168,000 on PWCC under the headline that the card "may be crashing in price". A CGC 10 is not the same as a PSA 10, and the two often sell at different levels, but the sale showed how far top-grade prices had moved from the 2022 peak.

What is similar, and what is different

  • The speed. In 2020 to 2022, the card's price rose roughly twentyfold over about five years. In 2026, the reported price more than doubled within a single year.
  • The supply. Then and now, the PSA 10 population is close to 120 copies. Higher prices have not, so far, brought many new top-grade copies to light.
  • The breadth. The 2020 to 2022 rise was shared by a handful of famous cards: a Pikachu Illustrator sold for $900,000 in February 2022, a month before the Charizard record. The 2026 rise has come with record prices across many high-end Pokémon cards, including Japanese contest and tournament promos, which UltraBall says made up nine of the year's ten biggest sales.
  • The evidence. The 2022 record was a completed public auction sale. The 2026 figures are one reported sale without a named platform and one auction result listed as unpaid when checked. Records of this kind can be revised.

What to watch

The facts worth following are public. Whether the Fanatics Collect result is paid and confirmed will settle whether the card has a completed sale above $1 million. The next public sales will show whether $1 million is a new level or an isolated high: in 2022, the record price was followed by a long period without a higher one. The PSA population report shows whether more top-grade copies come forward at these prices. And the monthly count of six-figure Pokémon sales on the major auction platforms shows whether the wider high-end market is still widening or has started to narrow.

The 2022 precedent does not tell us what this card will do next. It shows that the last record set during a rapid run took almost four years to be beaten, and that the years in between were not a straight line. Signal Intelligence will keep tracking the facts as they are published.
