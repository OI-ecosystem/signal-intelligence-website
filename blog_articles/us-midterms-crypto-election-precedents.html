Democrats Won the Midterms. What Have US Elections Meant for Crypto Before?

7 Nov 2026 4 min read Signal Intelligence Research

US elections have mattered to crypto mainly through what Congress and regulators do next, and the last two showed how differently that can play out: a policy-driven rally after 2024, and an industry collapse unrelated to the vote in the same week as the 2022 midterms. The 2026 result, with Democrats taking both the House and the Senate, puts the stalled CLARITY Act at the centre of that question.

In this week's US midterm elections, Democrats won control of both the House of Representatives and the Senate. The new Congress, and with it two years of divided government under a Republican president, begins on 3 January 2027.

For crypto, the most immediate question is legislative. The CLARITY Act, the market structure bill meant to set out how digital assets are regulated in the US, passed the House in July 2025. In the Senate it failed to clear the 60-vote procedural threshold, losing 49 to 50, and it is now described as stalled. Unless the Senate acts first, the bill expires when the current Congress ends on 3 January 2027. Coverage after the vote expects Maxine Waters to be in line to chair the House Financial Services Committee, and industry groups expect a tougher tone on crypto from the new majorities.

The natural question is whether an election like this has moved crypto before, and what came next. Two recent elections give useful, and very different, reference points.

2024: an election read as a policy change

The 2024 presidential election is the clearest case of a vote being read by the crypto market as a policy event. According to Reuters, bitcoin rose above $100,000 for the first time on 5 December 2024, up about 45% in the four weeks after Donald Trump's election victory. Reuters linked the move to expectations that the new administration would create a friendlier regulatory environment, and noted that Trump had just said he would nominate Paul Atkins, a former SEC commissioner, to run the Securities and Exchange Commission. More than $4 billion had flowed into US-listed bitcoin exchange-traded funds over the same period.

The policy follow-through came the next summer. On 18 July 2025, President Trump signed the GENIUS Act, the first major federal law governing cryptocurrency, which sets rules for stablecoins and makes it easier for banks to issue them. The House passed the CLARITY Act that same week. The Senate step for CLARITY is the one that never came.

2022: the midterm week that crypto remembers for something else

The 2022 midterms were held on 8 November 2022. Republicans won control of the House and Democrats kept the Senate. For crypto, though, that week is remembered for an industry event, not the vote. On 11 November 2022, the crypto exchange FTX filed for bankruptcy protection in the US and its founder and chief executive, Sam Bankman-Fried, resigned. The filings said FTX US and Alameda Research had between $10 billion and $50 billion in liabilities, and CoinDesk reported that bitcoin fell by more than $1,000 within minutes of the news, to about $16,500.

The lesson from 2022 is a cautionary one. Election weeks attract attention, but the biggest move in crypto that week came from inside the industry. Reading every November move as a reaction to the vote would have got that week wrong.

What is similar, and what is different

  • Direction of policy. In 2024 the election result was read as clearing the way for crypto-friendly rules, and a law did follow. In 2026 the stated expectation is the opposite: tougher oversight and a harder path for pending legislation.
  • What is at stake. In 2024 the market was pricing hopes for future rules. In 2026 there is a specific bill, CLARITY, with a clear deadline of 3 January 2027.
  • What is already in place. Unlike in 2022 or 2024, a federal stablecoin law, the GENIUS Act, is already on the books, so not everything depends on the new Congress.
  • Divided government. 2022 produced a split Congress; 2026 produces a Congress controlled by the party that does not hold the White House. Both make sweeping new legislation harder, which can mean a slower pace in either direction.

What to watch

The facts worth following over the next two months are concrete. Does the Senate take up the CLARITY Act again before 3 January 2027, or does the bill lapse? Who chairs the House Financial Services Committee and the Senate Banking Committee in the new Congress, and what do they say about digital assets? And how do regulators carry on putting the GENIUS Act into effect while Congress changes hands?

Elections have coincided with sharp crypto moves before, but the precedents point in different directions, and in 2022 the defining event was not political at all. Past elections do not tell us how markets will respond this time. Signal Intelligence will keep tracking the facts as they develop.
