Anthropic Slipped Below Its IPO Price, Then Climbed Back. Facebook Took 14 Months; Uber Took Four Weeks.

28 Nov 2026 5 min read Signal Intelligence Research

Anthropic’s shares closed below their $83 offering price on 24 November, the fourth day of trading, and were back above it by Friday 27 November, closing at $91.88. Two earlier high-profile technology listings that broke issue took far longer: Uber needed almost four weeks to trade back at its offer price in 2019, and Facebook needed more than 14 months to close above its own in 2013.

Anthropic made its Nasdaq debut on Thursday 19 November 2026, after pricing its shares at $83 and raising $81.3 billion, the largest IPO on record, according to Forbes. The first trade came at $111.50, about 34% above the offering price, and the shares briefly topped $115 on the first day, Yahoo Finance reported.

The premium did not last. On Tuesday 24 November the shares fell below $83 for the first time, and they closed that day at $81.91, according to Yahoo Finance. Coverage at the time did not identify a single company-specific reason; reports pointed instead to a broad retreat in technology shares and the unwinding of the first-day premium. Yahoo Finance noted that the IPO had valued the company at roughly 30 times trailing sales. On Wednesday 25 November the stock traded as low as $73.85, according to Unite.AI's record of Nasdaq data, a figure that matches the low of the trading range shown by the Motley Fool.

Then the market turned. On Friday 27 November, a shortened session after Thanksgiving, Anthropic's shares rose 10.98% to close at $91.88, back above the offering price, the Motley Fool reported, as buyers returned to AI stocks across the market. Reuters reported that the Nasdaq Composite gained 2.31% that day, but that the S&P 500 and the Nasdaq were still on track for their worst November since 2008; Reuters also cited Anthropic's brief fall below its IPO price as one of the sources of volatility across the AI trade.

So the natural question is: what happened to earlier large technology listings that fell below their offer price, and how long did they take to get back? Two well-known cases give very different answers.

Facebook, 2012: more than 14 months below $38

Facebook's first trading day, 18 May 2012, was marred by technical glitches on the Nasdaq stock market, the Associated Press reported. The shares had been priced at $38, and they ended that first day at $38.23. Worries about the company's growth prospects then drove a long slide, and the stock fell as low as $17.55 in September 2012, according to CNN and the AP.

The recovery came with evidence from the business itself. In late July 2013 Facebook reported second-quarter revenue of $1.81 billion, up 53% on a year earlier, NPR reported, and mobile advertising made up 41% of its advertising revenue, according to CNN. On Wednesday 31 July 2013 the shares rose above $38 for the first time since the IPO, but the gain faded and they closed at $36.80. Two days later, on Friday 2 August 2013, Facebook closed above its IPO price for the first time, at $38.05, more than 14 months after its debut, the AP reported.

Uber, 2019: four weeks under the offer price

Uber priced its IPO at $45 a share in May 2019, at the lower end of its targeted range of $44 to $50, raising $8.1 billion and taking a market value of $82 billion, the AP reported. The AP said the cautious pricing may have reflected doubts about whether ride-hailing could make money, after Lyft's listing six weeks earlier, along with worries about a US trade war with China.

The shares opened at $42 on 10 May 2019, and TechCrunch reported that over the following four weeks they hovered between $40 and $43. On Wednesday 5 June 2019 Uber closed up 5% at $45, trading at its IPO price for the first time, TechCrunch reported. The turn came a day after the end of the post-IPO quiet period, when several of the underwriting banks began coverage with positive ratings, and after first-quarter results that landed at or near the top of the ranges Uber had given in its prospectus, according to TechCrunch and the Motley Fool.

What is similar, and what is different

  • Speed. Anthropic spent only a few sessions below its offer price before closing above it again. Uber took almost four weeks to get back to $45, and Facebook more than 14 months to close above $38.
  • The start. Anthropic opened well above its offer price, at $111.50, before falling back. Uber opened below its offer price, and Facebook closed its first day only just above it, after a debut hit by trading problems.
  • The market backdrop. Anthropic's fall came during what Reuters described as a broad sell-off in AI shares, and its recovery came on a day when the whole sector rallied. Uber's listing came during worries about trade with China; Facebook's slide was driven mainly by doubts about its own business.
  • What turned the tide. For Facebook and Uber, the recoveries followed company news: quarterly results, mobile advertising and analyst coverage. Anthropic's rebound on 27 November coincided with a market-wide move rather than a company announcement.

Anthropic is not the only recent giant listing to test its offer price. Forbes noted that SpaceX, whose IPO earlier in 2026 raised $75 billion, fell below its $135 IPO price in July.

What to watch

The useful questions are factual ones. Does Anthropic's share price stay above $83 once the broader AI market settles, or was Friday's move part of a sector-wide bounce? Reuters reported that traders are almost fully pricing in a Federal Reserve rate rise in December, which bears on all richly valued growth shares. And, as with Facebook and Uber, the end of the quiet period and the company's first quarterly results as a listed company are the points at which investors get new information about the business itself.

Earlier listings do not tell us how this one will go. Facebook and Uber show that the first weeks after an IPO can be a poor guide to what follows, in either direction. Signal Intelligence will keep tracking the facts as they come in.
