Ether Is Two-Thirds Below Its Record. It Has Fallen Further Twice Before.

23 Nov 2026 4 min read Signal Intelligence Research

Ether traded at about $1,680 on 21 November, roughly two-thirds below its August 2025 record of about $4,947. It has fallen further twice before, by 94% in 2018 and 81% in 2022, and each time it took roughly three to four years to regain its previous peak.

Ether has had a harder autumn than bitcoin. As US stocks and crypto sold off together last week, the second-largest cryptocurrency fell further from its record, and a question that last came up in 2022 is back: has ether been this far down before, and what came next?

What is happening now

Fortune put ether at $1,730.91 on the morning of 20 November, down 3.94% in a day and 27.85% lower than a month earlier, when it stood at $2,399.06. CoinDesk reported ether at about $1,680 on 21 November, after a weekend in which bitcoin touched an early low of $55,130 before rebounding above $60,000. CoinDesk, citing CoinGlass data, reported about $1.8 billion of leveraged positions liquidated in 24 hours, $1.3 billion of them bets on rising prices.

Against ether's record, the fall is large. CoinDesk reported an intraday high of around $4,946.90 on 24 August 2025. At $1,680, ether is about 66% below that level.

The backdrop is mostly outside crypto. CoinDesk reported that the Nasdaq fell 10.4% last week, its largest weekly drop since the March 2020 Covid crash, and that markets now expect a quarter-point rise in US interest rates in December. The Block linked the selling to a downturn led by AI-related shares and to those rate expectations.

Money has also been leaving the US spot ether funds. The Block reported $398.9 million of outflows in the week to 14 November, the seventh negative week in a row, leaving the funds about $804.5 million in net outflows for 2026. A further $314 million left the following week, the eighth consecutive week of withdrawals.

Two other features of today's market have no real equivalent in earlier cycles. Bitmine Immersion Technologies, a listed company that holds ether as a treasury asset, said it held 6,077,745 ETH on 15 November, 4.97% of the 122.2 million ether in existence. And the hacker behind the $6.3 billion theft from Binance on 29 October, which the FBI has attributed to North Korea, is still moving coins: Decrypt reported that wallets linked to the hack moved 74,880 ETH, about $136.8 million, on 17 November, according to Arkham.

2018: a 94% fall and three years to recover

Ether's first big boom ended in January 2018, at a record of about $1,431 by CoinDesk's figures. The decline that followed was long and deep. In early December 2018 CoinDesk reported that ether had slipped to $81.30, down 94% from that January peak.

The recovery came, but slowly. The Block reported that ether broke its previous record of $1,420 on 19 January 2021, reaching about $1,423 on Coinbase, three years after the 2018 peak.

2022: an 81% fall and almost four years to recover

The second boom peaked in November 2021, at a record of $4,878. Through the first half of 2022 ether fell hard. Kitco reported that on 18 June 2022 ether dropped to a low of $893, down 81% from its November high.

The years that followed brought two notable events. Ethereum moved to proof-of-stake in the Merge on 15 September 2022, which Kitco reported was completed at block 15537393. Two months later, the collapse of the FTX exchange brought fresh selling: The Block reported that ether lost about a quarter of its value in the week FTX unravelled. Even in April 2025, Cointelegraph reported ether at $1,410, its lowest level since March 2023.

The record was finally broken on 22 August 2025. Decrypt reported a new high of $4,879 against the previous record of $4,878 from November 2021, almost four years earlier. Two days later ether set the current record, near $4,947.

What is similar, and what is different

  • Depth. Ether is about 66% below its record today. In 2018 it fell 94% and in 2022 81% before turning, so today's fall is not yet as deep as either earlier one.
  • Interest rates. This fall has come with expectations of higher US rates and the Nasdaq's worst week since March 2020, so the pressure is coming from wider markets as much as from crypto itself.
  • Who holds ether. Today there are US spot ether funds and listed companies such as Bitmine holding nearly 5% of supply. In 2018 and 2022 neither existed, which means there are new channels for both buying and selling.
  • Time to recover. In both earlier cycles ether took roughly three to four years to regain its old peak. That is a record of what happened, not a timetable for what happens next.

What to watch

The checkable markers are the weekly flow figures for the US spot ether funds and whether the run of outflows breaks, Bitmine's published holdings, further movements from the Binance hack wallets, and the Federal Reserve's decision in December.

Ether has fallen further than this twice before, and both times it eventually set new records, though only after long waits and deeper falls. Earlier cycles happened in a different market, and none of them tells us what happens next. Signal Intelligence will keep tracking the facts as they develop.
