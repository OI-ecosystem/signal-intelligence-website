PSA Has Reopened Its Value Grading Tier. In 2021, It Took 14 Months to Return.

14 Nov 2026 5 min read Signal Intelligence Research

PSA reopened its Value grading tier on 29 October 2026 at $39.99 per card, just under five months after pausing it with about 10 million cards in its backlog. It has done this once before: in 2021 PSA suspended its cheaper services for the same reason, and Value only returned in June 2022, about 14 months later, after Express had come back $50 dearer.

On 28 October 2026, PSA said it would reopen its Value grading service the next day, 29 October, at 8 am Pacific time, in the US, Canada, Europe and Japan. The tier had been paused since 2 June. It returns at $39.99 per card, with a maximum insured value of $500 and an estimated turnaround of 90 to 100 business days (120 business days in Japan). Before the pause, Value cost $32.99 per card.

The reopening is partial. Only the Value tier is back; Value Bulk, the cheapest of the four paused tiers, remains closed. PSA put its backlog at 7.2 million cards at the time of the announcement, still above the 5 million it had set as its target when it paused the Value tiers. Ryan Hoge, president of PSA and Grading at Collectors, said: "We have added an incredible amount of capacity, but we still have work to do."

For Pokémon collectors this matters directly. TheGamer reported that much of the backlog came from the Pokémon boom, and the cost of grading is one of the main inputs when a collector decides whether a lower-value card is worth sending at all. This is also not the first time PSA has shut its cheaper tiers to deal with a flood of submissions.

How the 2026 pause unfolded

PSA announced the pause on 28 May 2026. It said a demand spike had pushed its queue to about 10 million cards, and Sports Collectors Digest reported a 20 percent increase in submissions in recent weeks that had added another 1.6 million cards. All four Value tiers, Value Bulk, Value, Value Plus and Value Max, were paused from 2 June. Regular, Express, Super Express and Walk-Through stayed open, with Regular's estimated turnaround extended from 40 to 50 days. PSA said it expected to take up to four months to bring the backlog down to 5 million, launched a public backlog tracker updated every two weeks, and extended Collectors Club memberships for the length of the pause.

The backlog fell more slowly than that estimate. On 9 September, PSA said its active global backlog was down to approximately 9.9 million units. On the same day it reshaped its menu: a new Standard tier at $59.99 per card, Regular renamed Priority at $79.99, Walk-Through renamed Premier at $599, and Express priced at $199. It also began accepting submissions in Europe through Frankfurt, citing a $200 million infrastructure investment and six global grading facilities.

By late October, Sports Illustrated reported, PSA's graded output was up nearly 50 percent in 2026 and 150 percent above its 2020 to 2025 weekly average. PSA said the 59 million items it had graded in the past three years exceeded the 57 million it graded in the 23 years before.

The precedent: the 2021 grading freeze

PSA has been here before. In a letter dated 30 March 2021, it told customers: "Effective immediately, PSA is temporarily suspending our Value, Regular and Express service levels." The suspended services covered price points from $20 to $150 per card; grading stayed open only for cards valued at $2,500 or more, starting at $300 per card. PSA said it hoped to restore the suspended services by 1 July.

The cause was similar: volume. Sports Collectors Digest later reported that PSA had been receiving more than 500,000 cards a week in early 2021. In July 2021, it described a backlog of as many as 10 million cards and waits of as long as nine months; later accounts put the peak higher, at more than 12 million.

The services came back in stages, and at higher prices. Express returned on 1 July 2021 at $200 per card, up from $150. PSA said it had devoted 98% of its capacity to the backlog over the previous three months and had increased capacity by 64% since the start of the year, but Economy remained suspended. The Value tier did not reopen until 9 June 2022, when PSA said it was close to catching up and that all existing backlog orders would be shipped before new Value orders were processed. From suspension to Value reopening, the 2021 freeze lasted about 14 months.

What is similar, and what is different

  • The trigger. Both pauses followed a surge in submissions that pushed the backlog to roughly 10 million cards or more.
  • The sequence. In both cases the cheapest services closed first and came back last, and higher tiers stayed open throughout.
  • The price on return. In 2021, Express came back $50 dearer. In 2026, Value has come back at $39.99, $7 more than before, and a new Standard tier sits above it.
  • The duration. The 2021 Value suspension lasted about 14 months. The 2026 pause lasted just under five months, although Value Bulk is still closed and the backlog remains above PSA's own target.
  • Capacity. PSA said in May 2026 that its capacity was up five times since 2021. It also began grading in Europe in September 2026. The 2026 backlog was cleared faster, but not as fast as PSA first estimated.

What to watch

The useful signals are factual and public. PSA's backlog tracker shows whether the queue keeps moving towards 5 million now that Value orders are coming back in. The reopening of Value Bulk would mark the full return of the cheapest service. Turnaround estimates for Value and Standard show whether reopened demand is outrunning the added capacity. And any further price changes will show where PSA sets the floor for grading lower-value cards.

The 2021 freeze does not tell us how the 2026 recovery will go. It shows that PSA has used tier pauses and staged reopenings to manage demand before, and that the cheaper services came back at higher prices. Signal Intelligence will keep tracking the facts as they are published.
