Jensen Huang Is Back at Nvidia After 11 Days. OpenAI and Disney Have Seen Sudden Returns Before.

24 Nov 2026 4 min read Signal Intelligence Research

Sudden returns of a former chief executive have happened before at large technology and media companies: OpenAI agreed to bring back Sam Altman four days after removing him in November 2023, and Disney's shares rose as much as 10% when it re-appointed Bob Iger in November 2022. Nvidia reinstated its co-founder Jensen Huang on 23 November 2026, eleven days after he resigned, and its shares rose more than 31% that day; these cases show how such comebacks have unfolded, not how this one will.

On Monday 23 November 2026, Nvidia's board announced that, effective immediately, Jensen Huang had been reinstated as president and chief executive and as a member of the board. The board voted unanimously, according to the company's release filed with the US Securities and Exchange Commission. Huang had resigned all three roles on 12 November, as we covered last week, and CBS News reported that the reversal came eleven days after his resignation.

The reversal was abrupt. CBS News reported that in an earnings call the previous week, the board chair, Harvey C. Jones, had said the company "does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO". The board's statement cited input from senior levels of the United States government, CBS reported. Colette Kress, who had served as interim chief executive, continues as chief financial officer, and Jones remains chair. The release also said a committee of independent directors is carrying out an investigation, that the board has formed a National Security and Export Control Committee, and that Nvidia is searching for a new chief compliance officer.

The market reaction was large. Reuters reported that Nvidia's shares soared more than 31% on Monday, adding more than $950 billion in market value and cutting the stock's year-to-date losses by two-thirds in a single session, while the Nasdaq 100 jumped nearly 7%. Short sellers faced about $12.2 billion in paper losses, their biggest one-day hit since at least 2019, according to ORTEX data cited by Reuters; ORTEX added that with only just over 1% of the float sold short, the move looked like a reaction to the news rather than a squeeze.

So the question readers are asking is a natural one: has a company brought back a departed chief executive this quickly before, and what came next? Two recent cases are useful.

OpenAI, 2023: four days from removal to return

The closest parallel in speed is OpenAI. On Friday 17 November 2023, the board of the non-profit that controls OpenAI said it had removed Sam Altman as chief executive, NBC News reported. Over the following days the company appointed two different interim chief executives, and Microsoft announced that Altman would lead a new AI research team there, according to Semafor and Al Jazeera.

Staff pressure was intense: NBC News reported that more than 740 of OpenAI's 770 employees signed a letter to the board asking for Altman's return. Late on Tuesday 21 November, OpenAI said it had reached "an agreement in principle" for Altman to return as chief executive, with a new initial board. Semafor reported that former Salesforce chief executive Bret Taylor would serve as chair, alongside former US Treasury Secretary Larry Summers, while Adam D'Angelo remained on the board.

OpenAI was a private company, so there was no share price to react. The return did, however, come with a reshaped board rather than simply a reversal of one decision.

Disney, 2022: a former chief executive brought back

The second case is a return after a longer absence. On the evening of Sunday 20 November 2022, Disney re-appointed Bob Iger as chief executive, effective immediately, replacing Bob Chapek, who stepped down, CNBC reported. Iger had led the company for 15 years, from 2005 to 2020, and signed on for two years.

The return came after a difficult year for the shares, which had fallen about 41% in 2022 up to the previous Friday's close, according to CNBC. On Monday 21 November, Disney's shares rose as much as 10% to $100.89, adding $17 billion in market value at the intraday high, Business Insider reported. Fortune noted that the shares had been up about 8% before the market opened.

What is similar, and what is different

  • The speed resembles OpenAI's. Nvidia's reversal took eleven days; OpenAI's took four. In both cases a board reversed course within days of a leadership change it had announced.
  • The governance changes differ. OpenAI's return came with a new initial board and a new chair. At Nvidia, the chair stays, and the board has instead added an investigation, a new committee and a search for a compliance officer.
  • The scale of the share move is different. Disney's shares rose as much as 10% on the day Iger returned, after a fall of about 41% that year. Nvidia's rise of more than 31% in one session, adding more than $950 billion in value, is of a different order, reflecting both the size of the company and how far its shares had fallen.
  • The reasons are not the same. Iger returned after a period of weak results under his successor; Altman returned after a board dispute and staff pressure. Nvidia's board cited input from the US government, which neither earlier case involved.

What to watch

The useful questions now are factual ones. What does the independent directors' investigation conclude, and when? Who becomes Nvidia's new chief compliance officer, and what does the new National Security and Export Control Committee publish about its work? And does the company deliver revenue in line with the guidance it gave the week before Huang's return?

Earlier comebacks do not tell us how this one will go, and each came about in very different circumstances. They do suggest that a reversal of a leadership change is rarely the end of the story: at OpenAI the board itself was rebuilt, and at Disney the returning chief executive took on a fixed term.
