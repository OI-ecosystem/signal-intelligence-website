30th Celebration Brings Back Base Set Charizard. Pokémon Has Done This Twice Before.

7 Nov 2026 4 min read Signal Intelligence Research

Yes: the 30th Celebration set is the third time in a decade that Pokémon has marked an anniversary by bringing back its most famous early designs, after Evolutions in 2016 and Celebrations in 2021. The precedents show how differently each set was built and sold, and the 2021 one arrived in a year so heated that a major US retailer temporarily stopped selling trading cards in its stores.

The Pokémon TCG: 30th Celebration expansion went on sale on 16 September 2026, the first set released on the same day worldwide. It is large by anniversary standards: a 199-card master set made up of 128 main-set cards, 30 numbered secret rares, three RGB Mew secret rares, a 30-card Classic Collection and eight Basic Energy cards. Every card in the set is foil, and the main set includes 30 different Pikachu cards, each drawn by a different artist.

The part that draws collectors' attention is the Classic Collection. It reprints thirty cards from across the game's history with their original set numbers, starting with Base Set Pikachu (58/102) and Base Set Charizard (4/102) and running through cards such as Shining Celebi, Dark Tyranitar, Metagross δ, Rayquaza EX and Arceus VSTAR. The November product wave keeps that theme going: the 30th Celebration Ultra-Premium Collection adds an exclusive pack with three etched vintage reprints alongside 29 booster packs.

So the obvious question is whether this has happened before, and what it looked like. It has, twice.

2016: Evolutions and the 20th anniversary

For the 20th anniversary, the Pokémon TCG released XY Evolutions in English on 2 November 2016. It was a 113-card set released to commemorate the twentieth anniversary, and in Japan it was sold as "Expansion Pack 20th Anniversary".

Evolutions did not reprint the 1999 cards as they were. It rebuilt Base Set cards in the modern format, keeping most of the original card layout while updating HP, attacks and abilities so the cards could be played under the rules of the day. In other words, it was a playable expansion that borrowed the look of the original set, rather than a collection of facsimiles.

2021: Celebrations and the 25th anniversary

Five years later, Celebrations took a different approach. Released on 8 October 2021, it paired a 25-card main set with a 25-card Classic Collection of reprinted older cards, including Base Set Charizard, Umbreon ☆ and Tapu Lele-GX. The Classic Collection cards are not playable in competitive formats. Celebrations was also sold only inside collection boxes, tins and premium products: individual booster packs were not on sale.

The anniversary year had started months earlier. In February 2021, a McDonald's Happy Meal promotion offered 25 cards to collect, 50 in total counting holo and non-holo versions. By May, trading cards had become a safety concern for at least one major retailer. After a fight in the car park of a Target store in Brookfield, Wisconsin, which local reporting linked to a dispute over trading cards, Target said it would "temporarily suspend the sale of MLB, NFL, NBA and Pokémon trading cards within our stores" from 14 May 2021. The cards stayed on sale online, and Target did not say when in-store sales would resume. When Celebrations was announced in June 2021, coverage noted that some retailers had temporarily halted card sales, citing employee and customer safety.

What is similar, and what is different

The three anniversary sets share a common idea: bring back the cards that defined the early game, with Base Set Charizard and Pikachu at the centre. Beyond that, they differ in ways that matter for anyone trying to read the 2026 set through the earlier ones.

  • Format. Evolutions remade Base Set cards as playable modern cards. Celebrations and 30th Celebration both carry a separate Classic Collection of reprints that keep their original set numbers.
  • Size. Celebrations had 25 main-set cards and 25 classics. 30th Celebration has 128 main-set cards, 30 numbered secret rares and 30 classics, in a 199-card master set.
  • How it is sold. Celebrations was sold only in collection products, with no individual booster packs. 30th Celebration has its own booster packs, sold inside products such as the Ultra-Premium Collection.
  • Release. 30th Celebration launched on one day worldwide, a first for the game, while earlier sets reached different regions at different times; Evolutions, for example, came out in Japan in September 2016 and in English in November.
  • Market backdrop. Celebrations arrived in a year when safety concerns around trading cards led a national retailer to pull them from its store shelves. That backdrop was specific to 2021 and is not a given for any later set.

These differences are a reason for caution. A reprint set that is larger, sold in packs and released everywhere at once is a different product from a small, box-only set released in the middle of a collecting boom, even when both put Base Set Charizard in the box.

What to watch

For collectors following the anniversary, the useful questions are factual ones rather than predictions. How widely available are 30th Celebration products at retail through the November releases? How do the Classic Collection reprints trade relative to the main-set chase cards over time? And do grading services record large numbers of the reprints, which would tell us how many collectors treat them as items to keep rather than to open and play?

Past anniversary sets do not tell us how this one will behave. They do show that each anniversary has been built and sold differently, and that the conditions around a release, such as the 2021 demand surge, can matter as much as the cards themselves.
