Bitcoin Has Slipped Below Its 200-Week Average. Here Is What Followed Earlier Breaks.

15 Nov 2026 4 min read Signal Intelligence Research

Bitcoin fell below its 200-week moving average, around $68,000, on 13 November and kept falling, to about $63,500 late on 14 November. The line has been tested before: it held on a weekly-close basis in December 2018, was broken only briefly in March 2020, and in 2022 bitcoin stayed below it until October 2023, after a further fall to $15,480.

The 200-week moving average is simply the average of bitcoin's weekly closing prices over roughly the last four years. Because it moves slowly, many traders and analysts treat it as a long-term floor, and a break below it tends to draw attention. This week it broke again.

According to CoinDesk's live coverage on 13 November, bitcoin slipped below its 200-week moving average of around $68,000, touching $67,652 in the early hours. Bitfinex analysts quoted by Forbes put the line at around $67,800. Selling continued over the weekend: Bitcoin Magazine reported a fall of about 13% over the week, from near $75,750 on 7 November, and CryptoTicker, citing CoinGecko, reported bitcoin at $63,528 at 23:03 UTC on 14 November. That is roughly half its record of $126,198, set on 6 October 2025.

The reasons given are mostly about money, not crypto. Bitcoin Magazine reported that the US dollar index held at a 19-month high, that the 10-year Treasury yield closed Friday at 5.15%, and that traders had fully priced a quarter-point rise in US interest rates at the Federal Reserve's 8 to 9 December meeting, according to CME FedWatch. CoinDesk noted equities falling at the same time.

It is also not the first test of the line this year. Cointelegraph reported in August that bitcoin had dropped below the 200-week moving average, then at $64,216.

December 2018: the line held, just

After peaking at $19,783.06 on 17 December 2017, according to Fortune, bitcoin fell for a year. CoinDesk recorded the bear-market low at $3,122 on 15 December 2018. In January 2019 CoinDesk reported the 200-week average at $3,298 and noted that the support had held on a weekly closing basis in mid-December: the price traded near, and at times beneath, the line, but did not end a week below it.

That low proved to be the bottom of that cycle. A year later, CoinDesk reported, bitcoin was more than double its December 2018 low.

March 2020: a break that lasted days

The first clear break came during the Covid crash. CNBC reported on 13 March 2020 that bitcoin had lost 50% in two days, briefly dropping below $4,000 after starting the week above $9,000. K33 Research later dated that first move below the 200-week line to 12 March 2020, and CoinDesk has described it as a brief breakdown.

The recovery was fast. When the third halving arrived on 11 May 2020, CoinDesk put bitcoin at about $8,600, and Bloomberg reported that it vaulted above $29,000 to a new record on the last day of 2020. The cause of that crash was a sudden, global rush for cash, followed by very large central bank and government support.

June 2022: a break that lasted until October 2023

The 2022 case is the one most often cited this week, because it looked more like today. Bitcoin had set a record near $69,000 on 10 November 2021, as Cointelegraph reported, and then fell through the first half of 2022. BeInCrypto reported that bitcoin made a weekly close below its 200-week average in the week to 19 June 2022, with the line at about $22,700 and an intraday low of $17,605. K33 Research put the average at $22,427 and called it only the second time bitcoin had traded below it.

This time the break did not reverse quickly. After the collapse of the FTX exchange, Kitco reported bitcoin at a daily low of $15,480 on 21 November 2022, its lowest since November 2020. According to CoinDesk, bitcoin did not reclaim the 200-week line until October 2023.

What is similar, and what is different

  • Interest rates. Like 2022, this week's move came with rising yields, a strong dollar and expectations of higher US rates. In 2020 the shock was a one-off rush for cash that was met with heavy policy support.
  • The level of the line. The 200-week average now sits far higher than in earlier cycles, around $68,000 against about $22,427 to $22,700 in 2022, because it averages several years of much higher prices.
  • Repeated tests. Bitcoin has now gone below the line more than once in 2026. In 2018 the line held on weekly closes; in 2022 one break turned into a long stretch below it.
  • Market structure. Today's market includes US spot bitcoin funds. Bitcoin Magazine reported nine straight days of outflows from BlackRock's iShares Bitcoin Trust through Wednesday, about $2 billion in total, a channel for selling that did not exist in earlier cycles.

What to watch

The checkable facts are straightforward: whether bitcoin ends the coming weeks above or below the 200-week average on a weekly-close basis, what the Federal Reserve decides on 8 to 9 December, the direction of the dollar and long-term Treasury yields, and whether flows into the US spot funds turn around.

The earlier tests ended in very different ways: a bottom in 2018, a rapid rebound in 2020, and a long stretch below the line in 2022. A moving average is a description of past prices, not a forecast, and none of these episodes tells us what happens next. Signal Intelligence will keep tracking the facts as they develop.
