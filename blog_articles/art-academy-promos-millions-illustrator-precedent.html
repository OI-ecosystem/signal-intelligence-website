Art Academy Promo Cards Now Sell for Millions. Has a Contest Prize Card Done This Before?

18 Nov 2026 5 min read Signal Intelligence Research

Yes, once: the Pikachu Illustrator, a prize card from a 1998 illustration contest, went from $195,000 in 2019 to $16.49 million at auction in February 2026. The 2015 Art Academy contest promos have made a comparable jump in months rather than years, but each design was printed in a run of 100 copies and their sales record at these prices is only a few months old.

Two of the most talked-about Pokémon sales of the autumn were not cards anyone could buy in a shop. On 28 September 2026, a PSA 10 "Raincoat Pikachu", a 2015 Japanese XY promo from the Pokémon Art Academy contest, sold for $8.4 million including buyer's premium on Fanatics Collect, after 64 bids. Hypebeast and Kotaku both described it as the second most expensive Pokémon card ever sold.

A month later, at Goldin's 30th anniversary Pokémon auction on 25 October, a PSA 10 2015 Art Academy Daisuke Ochiai Mudkip sold for $4,880,000. Two more Art Academy promos in the same sale, Rina's Gourgeist and Iwaiya's Sableye, each sold for $976,000 in PSA 10. Sports Illustrated, using Card Ladder data, counted more than $6.8 million for the three Art Academy cards in that one auction.

The question collectors are asking is a simple one. Has a contest prize card climbed like this before, and what did that look like? There is one obvious precedent.

What the Art Academy cards are

The cards came from a 2015 competition tied to Pokémon Art Academy, a drawing game for the Nintendo 3DS. Entrants drew Pokémon in the game, and winning designs were printed as official cards. Hypebeast reported that Y. Fujishima won the "Dress-Up Pikachu" category, beating thousands of other entries, and that the prize was a run of 100 officially printed copies of his design. Sports Illustrated reported that there are exactly 100 of the Ochiai Mudkips, and that three of them are graded Gem Mint.

Until this year these were niche cards. Hypebeast reported PSA 10 Raincoat Pikachu sales on Fanatics Collect at $12,600 in July 2024 and $19,200 in June 2025. Kotaku reported that the previous high had been $156,250 at Heritage Auctions in June 2026, and that PSA lists 57 copies in PSA 10. The Mudkip moved just as quickly: a PSA 10 Ochiai Mudkip sold for $3,720,000 in Fanatics' September Premier Auction, according to Sports Illustrated and OTIA, before the $4.88 million result at Goldin.

The precedent: the Pikachu Illustrator

The Pikachu Illustrator is also a contest prize. Guinness World Records says it was never sold and was released only as a prize in a 1998 illustration contest, with 39 copies given to winners. It was designed by Atsuko Nishida.

Its rise took years, and it came in steps. Nintendo Wire and Kotaku reported that a PSA 9 copy sold for $195,000 in 2019. Kotaku reported a PSA 7 at $375,000 in February 2021. In July 2021, Logan Paul bought the only PSA 10 in a private deal in Dubai valued at $5,275,000, which Guinness recorded as the most expensive Pokémon card sold privately. In February 2022, a PSA 7 sold for $900,000 at Goldin. Then, in February 2026, Paul's PSA 10 sold at Goldin for $16,492,000 including buyer's premium, the most expensive trading card ever sold at auction, according to Guinness.

So between 2019 and 2026, the highest recorded Illustrator price went from $195,000 to $16.49 million, across three grades and both public and private sales. Prices also did not rise in a straight line between grades: a PSA 7 at $900,000 in 2022 sat far below the $5.275 million private PSA 10 deal of the year before.

A second reference: the Trophy Pikachu

Tournament prize cards have followed a similar path this year. Sports Illustrated reported that a PSA 8 Gold 1st Place Trophy Pikachu sold for $982,100 in March 2026. At the same Goldin auction where the Mudkip sold in October, a PSA 8 1997 Japanese Gold 1st Place Trophy Pikachu sold for $3,080,500. In May, a PSA 10 Bronze 3rd Place Trophy Pikachu had sold for $1.769 million through Goldin. Like the contest cards, the trophy cards were prizes, awarded to top finishers at early Japanese tournaments, not retail products.

What is similar, and what is different

  • Origin. Both the Illustrator and the Art Academy cards were prizes for drawing contests, not retail products.
  • Supply. Guinness puts the Illustrator at 39 copies, with one PSA 10. Each Art Academy design was printed in a run of 100, and Kotaku reports 57 Raincoat Pikachu copies in PSA 10. The Ochiai Mudkip is scarcer, with three Gem Mint copies.
  • Speed. The Illustrator went from $195,000 in 2019 to $16.49 million in 2026. Raincoat Pikachu went from $19,200 in June 2025 to $8.4 million in September 2026.
  • Depth of record. The Illustrator has public and private sales across several grades over many years. The Art Academy cards have only a handful of sales at these levels, all in the past few months. Kotaku noted that it was awaiting a response from Fanatics about the Raincoat Pikachu sale.
  • Market backdrop. In May 2026, Sports Illustrated cited Card Ladder data showing the Pokémon market up 176% year on year. HobbyCardIndex's November report showed the Pokémon category up 5.5% over the 30 days to 16 November.

What to watch

The useful questions are factual. Do further Art Academy copies sell at public auction, and at what grades? Does PSA's population of graded Art Academy cards grow as more holders submit copies? Do the other contest designs, not just Pikachu and Mudkip, trade at comparable levels? And does the wider Pokémon category keep rising, or do the top-end results stand apart from it?

The Illustrator does not tell us what the Art Academy cards will do next. It shows that a contest prize card can become one of the most valuable cards in the hobby, that it got there over several years and several sales, and that it did so with far fewer copies in existence.
