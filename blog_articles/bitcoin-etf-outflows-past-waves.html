Bitcoin ETFs Have Lost $2.35bn in November. Here Is What Followed Earlier Outflow Waves.

19 Nov 2026 5 min read Signal Intelligence Research

US spot bitcoin ETFs lost $2.35 billion in the first nine trading days of November, leaving them about $1.98 billion in net outflows for 2026. Earlier waves ended differently: bitcoin set new records within months of the January 2024 and early 2025 outflows, but was still lower a year after the record withdrawals of late 2025.

US spot bitcoin exchange-traded funds have had two weeks of heavy withdrawals in a row. That has turned their 2026 total from inflows to outflows, and raised a familiar question: when these funds have been drained before, what did bitcoin do next?

What is happening now

According to The Block, US spot bitcoin ETFs recorded $1.28 billion of net outflows in the week to 7 November, their worst week since late June. Only one day that week, Wednesday, brought money in. The funds had started the week with about $368 million of net inflows for 2026 and ended it with about $914.1 million of net outflows for the year.

The following week was nearly as heavy. The Block reported a further $1.07 billion of net outflows in the week to 14 November, taking the two-week total to $2.35 billion. A losing streak that began on 5 November had removed $1.91 billion by the end of that week. After nine trading days, November was already the funds' largest monthly outflow since June, when $4.51 billion left, and the 2026 deficit had grown to about $1.98 billion.

BlackRock's iShares Bitcoin Trust (IBIT), the largest fund, accounted for most of the Friday withdrawals in both weeks. On The Block's figures, IBIT held bitcoin worth $53.99 billion against $64.19 billion of cumulative inflows since launch, which means money put into the fund over its life is currently worth less than was invested.

The price has fallen alongside. The Block linked the first week's outflows to a selloff in crypto and equities after the US midterm elections, and the second to expectations of another Federal Reserve rate rise and the news of Jensen Huang's resignation as Nvidia chief executive. Fortune put bitcoin at $65,016.66 on the morning of 17 November, about 20% lower than a month earlier and about half its record of $126,198.07, set on 6 October 2025.

January 2024: the Grayscale exodus

The first big wave came days after the funds launched. Grayscale's GBTC, which converted from a trust into a spot ETF on 16 January 2024, saw heavy redemptions. NYDIG reported on 26 January that redemptions had reached $4.8 billion, and Cointelegraph reported that bitcoin fell from $48,700 to $38,600 in a matter of days.

Two differences stand out. First, much of the selling came from specific holders unwinding old positions: NYDIG noted shares tied to the bankrupt FTX estate, worth about $900 million. Second, the new funds were taking money in at the same time, so total spot ETF flows stayed positive, at $733 million since launch by NYDIG's count. Within two months, Bloomberg reported, bitcoin had reached a record $73,664 on 13 March 2024, as daily net inflows into the funds topped $1 billion for the first time.

February and March 2025: "hot money" leaves

The second wave was broader. Bloomberg reported that investors pulled a record $3.3 billion from US spot bitcoin ETFs in February 2025, as bitcoin slid as much as 28% from its record on the day of the presidential inauguration; The Defiant put the month at $3.65 billion, and the figures vary with the data provider. Over February and March together, CoinDesk later reported, $4.32 billion left the funds.

That wave also turned. The Block reported that bitcoin hit a low of $76,320 on 8 April 2025, crossed back above $100,000 on 8 May, and set a new record of around $109,400 on 21 May, as risk assets recovered from their April lows.

November and December 2025: a turn that did not reverse

The third wave is the most recent, and it ended differently. CoinMarketCap, citing SoSoValue data, reported $3.48 billion of net outflows in November 2025, the largest monthly withdrawal since February, with IBIT recording its largest daily outflow since launch, $523 million, on 18 November. Morningstar reported that bitcoin was down 28% from its October peak and fell to a low near $84,000 late in the month.

CoinDesk reported a further $1.09 billion of outflows in December, a combined $4.57 billion over two months, the largest since the funds' debut, alongside a 20% slide in bitcoin's price. This time there was no quick return to a record. A year on, Fortune's figures show bitcoin about 29% lower than on 17 November 2025, when it stood at $92,157.44.

What was similar, and what was different

  • Price came first, or together. In each case, large outflows arrived during a sharp fall in price rather than clearly ahead of it. Flows describe what investors did; on their own they have not marked tops or bottoms.
  • Who was selling. In January 2024 the outflows were concentrated in one older product while new funds gathered money. In 2025 and now, the withdrawals have been spread across the funds and led by IBIT.
  • The backdrop. The 2025 spring recovery came as wider risk assets rebounded. Today's outflows have come alongside falling equities and expectations of another Federal Reserve rate rise.
  • Scale. November 2026 is not yet a record: $2.35 billion after nine trading days, against $4.51 billion in June 2026 and the $4.57 billion two-month run at the end of 2025.

What to watch

The checkable markers are the daily and weekly flow figures published by the issuers and trackers such as SoSoValue, whether the losing streak breaks, the Federal Reserve's December decision, and whether IBIT's holdings move back above the money invested in it.

The three earlier waves ended in three different ways: a record within two months in 2024, a record within three months in 2025, and a lower price a year later after the end of 2025. Fund flows are a record of past decisions, not a forecast, and none of these episodes tells us what happens next. Signal Intelligence will keep tracking the facts as they develop.
